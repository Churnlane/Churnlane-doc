These are the questions that come up most often from teams in the Churnlane beta. We've answered them directly rather than routing them through a support flow, because they deserve straight answers. If something here doesn't answer your question, there's a note at the bottom on how to reach us.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How long before it tells me something useful?">
    It depends on the signal type. Connect Stripe (or Paddle or Chargebee) and billing risk signals — failed cards, plan downgrades, billing threshold crossings — are available the **same day**. There's no lag because the data is already historical.

    Usage signals take a little longer. Churnlane builds its behavioral baselines by comparing week-on-week activity, which means you need about **a week of data** before usage-based churn scores become reliable. During that window, the dashboard shows you what it knows (billing signals) and tells you what it's still learning (usage baselines), rather than showing you a number it can't stand behind.

    After the first week, scores update hourly and reflect the full picture.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Will it email my customers without asking me?">
    No. By default, **everything waits in your approval queue**. Churnlane will propose an action and explain its reasoning, but it will not send anything to a customer until you approve it.

    Autonomous mode — where Churnlane executes actions without per-action approval — exists and works well, but you turn it on deliberately, one action type at a time. Even then, you can set hard limits on what autonomous actions are allowed to do: the maximum discount percentage, the largest credit, which customers are eligible for a free month. The system will never exceed those limits, and anything that would exceed them routes back to the approval queue.

    The default answer to "will it do something I didn't intend?" is no.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Where does it get the data?">
    From the tools you connect — Churnlane doesn't use third-party data, scraped data, or anything you haven't explicitly plugged in.

    Specifically:

    - **Billing signals** come from Stripe, Paddle, or Chargebee, depending on which you connect
    - **Product and support signals** come from Intercom, PostHog, or HubSpot if you connect them
    - **Direct usage events** come from the Churnlane SDK, which you instrument in your product to send the events that matter to your churn model

    If you haven't connected a source, Churnlane doesn't have access to it. You can see every connected data source at any time in **Settings → Integrations**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What about customers who leave anyway?">
    Churnlane tracks churned and retained customers separately, and it's careful about attribution. Revenue that churns despite an intervention is logged as lost — it isn't counted as saved. Nothing is claimed twice.

    In your dashboard, you'll see a week-by-week breakdown of:

    - **Retained MRR** — customers who were flagged as at-risk and stayed
    - **Lost MRR** — customers who churned, regardless of whether an intervention was attempted
    - **Uncontested churn** — customers who left without a churn score crossing the intervention threshold (useful for calibrating your alert sensitivity)

    This means the revenue numbers Churnlane shows you are conservative by design. If you see $4,200 in retained MRR this week, that's real retention you can attribute to an intervention — not a vanity metric.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is my data secure?">
    Yes. A few specifics worth knowing:

    - Your **secret API key** is used only server-side in your own infrastructure. It never reaches the browser and is never transmitted to Churnlane's servers.
    - Churnlane **does not use cookies** on your customers' sessions and does not attach any tracking scripts to your product's frontend.
    - Churnlane **does not record sessions**, capture keystrokes, or read form inputs.
    - Data in transit is encrypted with TLS. Data at rest is encrypted in Churnlane's infrastructure.

    If you're on Scale, your workspace runs in a **dedicated environment** — your data is not co-mingled with other tenants at the infrastructure level.

    If you have specific security or compliance requirements (SOC 2, GDPR data processing agreements, etc.), contact support@churnlane.com and we'll provide the relevant documentation.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I use Churnlane with multiple products or workspaces?">
    Each Churnlane workspace tracks one product. The workspace is scoped to a single set of integrations, a single customer list, and a single set of automation rules — this keeps the churn model clean and avoids the signal mixing that happens when multiple products share a workspace.

    If you run multiple products and want to track them separately, you'll need a workspace per product. Contact **support@churnlane.com** to discuss multi-product setups — the team can walk you through the options and how billing works across workspaces.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if I exceed my plan's customer limit?">
    Churnlane will send you an alert when you're approaching your tracked customer limit — typically at 80% and again at 95% of your plan's ceiling. You'll have time to upgrade before hitting the limit.

    If you do exceed the limit, **Churnlane won't drop any data mid-month**. Your existing tracking continues uninterrupted. New customers above the limit won't receive churn scores until you upgrade, but nothing already in the system is lost or degraded.

    Upgrade at any time from **Settings → Billing**, or contact support@churnlane.com if you'd like to discuss which plan makes sense for your current growth trajectory.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
