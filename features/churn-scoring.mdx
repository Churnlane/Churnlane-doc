Churnlane assigns every account a churn risk score that refreshes every hour. Unlike tools that compare your customers to an industry average, Churnlane measures each account against its own historical behaviour — so a power user who logs in twice a week looks very different from a light user who logs in twice a month, even if both show the same absolute activity level. When something slips from that account's normal, the score moves and you get an explanation.

## How Scores Work

Scores run from **0 to 100**. Higher means higher risk. Every hour, Churnlane re-runs the full scoring model for every account in your portfolio using that account's own baseline as the reference point.

| Principle | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Range** | 0 (no signal) → 100 (critical risk) |
| **Frequency** | Re-scored every hour |
| **Baseline** | Each account compared to its own historical normal |
| **Threshold** | Scores above 70 need your attention |

Because scores are account-relative, a new customer ramping up usage and a mature customer whose usage suddenly drops are evaluated on completely different baselines. The model adapts as an account's normal evolves over time.

## Reading Your Score

Use the bands below to prioritise your workload. A score doesn't mean a customer will churn — it means something has shifted enough to deserve a closer look.

| Band | Score | What It Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | 0 – 30 | Account is behaving within its normal range. No action required. |
| **Moderate** | 31 – 70 | Some signals have moved. Worth a check-in but not urgent. |
| **High** | 71 – 100 | One or more signals have shifted significantly. **Act now.** |

<Note>
  Anything over 70 needs you. Churnlane surfaces these accounts at the top of your portfolio view and can trigger automated playbooks or alerts the moment a score crosses the threshold.
</Note>

## Plain-English Explanations

Every score comes with a one-sentence explanation written in plain English — no model jargon, no raw feature weights. Below the headline sentence, Churnlane lists the ranked evidence that contributed to the score, each with a percentage share of the overall signal.

**Example explanation**

> *"This account's login frequency dropped 60% over the past 14 days while support tickets increased."*

**Example evidence list**

| Signal | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Login frequency (–60% vs. baseline) | 44% |
| Support ticket volume (+3 open tickets) | 31% |
| Feature usage — data export (0 events, 21-day gap) | 15% |
| Seat activity (2 of 8 seats active this week) | 10% |

The explanation and evidence update every time the score is recalculated. You always know not just the number, but the reason behind it.

## Dashboard Metrics

The portfolio dashboard shows four headline metrics at all times. These numbers update as scores change and as billing events come in.

| Metric | What It Shows |
|---|---|
| **Tracked MRR** | Total monthly recurring revenue across all scored accounts. |
| **At-Risk MRR** | Combined MRR of every account currently in the At Risk or Critical band. |
| **Kept MRR (90 days)** | MRR retained from accounts that were At Risk or Critical and did not churn in the past 90 days. |
| **Invoices Recovered** | Count and value of failed invoices that were successfully collected through payment recovery. |

<Tip>
  Use **At-Risk MRR** in your weekly CS standup to size the revenue exposure before it becomes a pipeline problem. When Kept MRR trends up, your retention plays are working.
</Tip>

## Signals Used

Churnlane draws on five categories of signals when calculating each score. Every signal is evaluated relative to that account's own history.

<Accordion title="Login Frequency">
  How often users in the account authenticate, measured per seat and in aggregate. A sustained drop — especially across multiple seats — is one of the strongest leading indicators of churn.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Seat Activity">
  The proportion of provisioned seats that have generated any product event in the current period. Low seat utilisation often predicts a renewal conversation where the buyer questions the per-seat price.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Feature Usage">
  Which features have been used, how recently, and how often. Churnlane tracks both breadth (how many features) and depth (how frequently). A sudden gap in a previously active workflow is a strong signal.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Support Sentiment">
  The volume and tone of open and recent support tickets. High ticket volume combined with negative sentiment shifts the score upward even when product usage looks healthy.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Billing Events">
  Failed payments, dunning states, downgrade requests, and invoice disputes. Billing friction is a direct signal and is weighted accordingly in the model.
</Accordion>
